The Weaver’s Debt

Their happily ever after was a lie. The Weaver’s Debt begins where an inherited story should have ended and asks what happens when the promises of a familiar tale become obligations carried forward.As buried choices return, folklore becomes intimate and unsettling. The novel turns inheritance into a moral pressure: what one generation refuses to name can become the burden of the next. Familiar story architecture is treated not as comfort but as evidence—of promises made, consequences deferred and the cost of allowing inherited narratives to speak for the people trapped inside them.

Historical literary fiction · Published 2025

By Blood,
Ink & Salt

A historical reimagining of Shek Ying—Zheng Yi Sao—and the red-sailed world she helped reshape. Moving through a maritime society where authority is negotiated through codes, loyalties, memory and force, the novel asks who gets written into history and who disappears between its lines.Power is never abstract here: it is carried by people, rules, debts and the stories survivors choose to preserve. The result is a portrait of a woman navigating a world in which survival, command and authorship of one’s own history are inseparable. The sea becomes both political territory and archive: a place where reputation, allegiance and written codes determine who is remembered, who is feared and who is allowed to tell the story afterward.

03 · Ten volumes

The Hadal Cycle.

A ten-volume speculative arc from the hadal trench to Mars, Europa and a transformed Earth—built around memory, pressure, adaptation and the changing definition of humanity.

≋

The ocean does not simply conceal the past. Across ten novels, the Hadal Cycle follows what happens when biology begins to remember, systems begin to listen, and adaptation moves from the deep ocean into civilisation, Mars and eventually the living architecture of Earth itself.

The Listening Tide

Across Earth, Mars and Europa, the same impossible rhythm appears in living roots, dust spirals and sub-ice chimes. Coral, Thal, Ori, Roche and Freedman discover that the pattern behaves less like a transmitted message than a memory becoming structural.

Verdant Ascension

04 · About

The page came first.

Writing across history, science and the spaces where certainty begins to fracture.

Frederick Samuel grew up in the quiet corners of his father’s Sri Lankan printing press, where stories were not spoken but pressed into paper.

That early encounter with language shaped a lifelong fascination with the space between words and what they leave unsaid. His path has crossed continents, while writing remained a constant: a private act of preservation shaped by memory and myth.

His fiction ranges from historical reimaginings to speculative work about technology, identity and adaptation, but repeatedly returns to the same questions: what do people remember, what do systems erase, and what survives after power has moved on?

Stories should not merely end. They should leave a pressure behind.
